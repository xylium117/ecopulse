How EcoPulse AI Works

Planetary Multi-Spectral Remote Sensing, SAR Radar & Neural Spatio-Temporal Segmentation
An "MRI Scanner" for Planet Earth Standard optical photographs only show visible light. When dense monsoon storm clouds blanket Nepal or smoke envelopes a wildfire in California, optical sensors get obscured.

EcoPulse fuses Sentinel-2 Multi-Spectral (NIR, Red, SWIR) with Sentinel-1 C-Band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR). Radar microwaves easily penetrate cloud covers, while multi-spectral bands isolate chlorophyll loss, soil moisture saturation, and flood water expansion in real time.
RED (665 nm) & SWIR (2190 nm)
Thermal & Burn Severity
Short-wave infrared penetrates smoke. Used in Normalized Burn Ratio ($\text{NBR}$) to segment charcoal scars.
GREEN (560 nm) & MNDWI
Water Index Extraction
Water strongly reflects visible green light while completely absorbing SWIR light, creating peak contrast for flood detection.
SENTINEL-1 SAR RADAR (C-Band)
Cloud-Penetrating Inundation
Smooth flood waters cause specular reflection, sending microwave energy away from the satellite. This causes a steep drop in backscatter ($\sigma^0$).
Mathematical Formulations & Hazard Indices
1. Flash Flood Susceptibility Index (FFSI) Cloudburst Runoff Risk
$$\text{FFSI} = \alpha \cdot \text{Saturation} + \beta \cdot \Delta P_{72\text{h}} + \gamma \cdot \text{CN} \qquad (\text{Amber Warning Signal})$$
Integrates antecedent soil saturation, 72-hour precipitation anomaly, and runoff curve number to paint flash flood risk.
2. SAR Radar Specular Drop & Inundation Extent Standing Flood Water
$$\Delta\sigma^0 = \sigma^0_{\text{post}} - \sigma^0_{\text{pre}} \quad [\text{dB}] \qquad \text{MNDWI} = \frac{\text{Green} - \text{SWIR}}{\text{Green} + \text{SWIR}}$$
A backscatter attenuation of $\Delta\sigma^0 \le -6.5\text{ dB}$ paired with $\text{MNDWI} > 0.12$ isolates deep submerged floodplains in luminous electric blue.
3. Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) Canopy Greenness & Photosynthesis
$$\text{NDVI} = \frac{\text{NIR} - \text{Red}}{\text{NIR} + \text{Red}}$$
Ranges from $-1.0$ (water) to $+1.0$ (dense rainforest canopy). Stressed plants fall between $0.20$ and $0.45$.
4. Normalized Burn Ratio (NBR) & Burn Severity ($\Delta\text{NBR}$) Wildfire Severity
$$\text{NBR} = \frac{\text{NIR} - \text{SWIR}}{\text{NIR} + \text{SWIR}} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \Delta\text{NBR} = \text{NBR}_{\text{pre}} - \text{NBR}_{\text{post}}$$
$\Delta\text{NBR} > 0.44$ indicates high burn severity where mature canopy biomass was converted into charcoal and ash.
How the Spatio-Temporal Neural Network Works (4 Steps)
STEP 1: MULTI-MODAL INGESTION
Time-Stacked Tensors
The model loads multi-spectral tiles across 2 time steps ($T_0$ pre-event baseline and $T_1$ post-event pass) as a $5\text{D}$ tensor: $(B, T=2, H=256, W=256, C=3)$.
STEP 2: SPATIAL ENCODER
2D Convolutions
Shared $32 \to 64 \to 128$ convolutional filters extract riverbeds, terrain contours, embankments, and canopy structure independently for each image.
STEP 3: TEMPORAL MEMORY
ConvLSTM2D Delta Gating
A recurrent convolutional memory cell isolates sudden water expansion and thermal burns while ignoring harmless seasonal variations.
STEP 4: DAMAGE MAP OUTPUT
U-Net Transposed Decoder
Upsamples features with skip connections to output a precise $256 \times 256$ damage probability mask, vectorised into glowing GeoJSON damage polygons on the map.
