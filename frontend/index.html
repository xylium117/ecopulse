How EcoPulse AI Works

Planetary Multi-Spectral Remote Sensing & Neural Time-Series Segmentation
An "MRI Scanner" for Planet Earth Standard RGB photographs only show what human eyes can see. But when a wildfire burns or illegal loggers cut down a rainforest, smoke, clouds, and seasonal shadows can hide the damage.

Satellites like Sentinel-2 and Landsat-9 capture invisible wavelengths of light: Near-Infrared (NIR) and Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR). EcoPulse feeds a time-stack of these satellite images ($T_0$ Before vs. $T_1$ After) into an AI neural network to detect forest loss, carbon release, and drought in real time.
RED LIGHT (665 nm)
Absorbed by Chlorophyll
Healthy green leaves absorb visible Red light for photosynthesis. When leaves die or burn, Red light reflection spikes upwards.
NEAR-INFRARED / NIR (842 nm)
Reflected by Cell Walls
Living spongy mesophyll cells act like bright mirrors to NIR light. Healthy forests glow intensely bright in NIR.
SHORT-WAVE IR / SWIR (2190 nm)
Moisture & Smoke Penetration
SWIR easily pierces through smoke and fog. It is heavily absorbed by water in plant leaves, but reflects brightly off dry charcoal and bare soil.
Mathematical Formulations & Index Definitions
1. Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) Canopy Greenness & Photosynthesis
$$\text{NDVI} = \frac{\text{NIR} - \text{Red}}{\text{NIR} + \text{Red}}$$
Ranges from $-1.0$ (water bodies) to $+1.0$ (dense rainforest canopy). Stressed plants fall between $0.20$ and $0.45$.
2. Normalized Burn Ratio (NBR) & Burn Severity ($\Delta\text{NBR}$) Wildfire Severity
$$\text{NBR} = \frac{\text{NIR} - \text{SWIR}}{\text{NIR} + \text{SWIR}} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \Delta\text{NBR} = \text{NBR}_{\text{pre}} - \text{NBR}_{\text{post}}$$
$\Delta\text{NBR} > 0.44$ indicates high burn severity where mature canopy biomass was converted into charcoal and ash.
3. Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) Agricultural Drought Stress
$$\text{VCI} = \frac{\text{NDVI}_{\text{current}} - \text{NDVI}_{\min}}{\text{NDVI}_{\max} - \text{NDVI}_{\min}} \times 100\%$$
Compares current canopy greenness against historical multi-year extremes. Values $< 35\%$ trigger moisture deficit advisories.
4. Estimated Carbon Emissions ($\text{CO}_2\text{ Flux}$) Biomass Loss Conversion
$$\text{Emissions (kt CO}_2) = \text{Area (ha)} \times \rho_{\text{biomass}} \times 0.47 \times \frac{44}{12} \times 10^{-3}$$
Where $\rho_{\text{biomass}} \approx 280\text{ t/ha}$ in tropical rainforests, $47\%$ is organic carbon content, and $\frac{44}{12}$ converts $\text{C}$ into $\text{CO}_2$.
How the AI Neural Network Thinks (4 Steps)
STEP 1: SENSOR INGESTION
Time-Stacked Tensors
The model loads multi-spectral tiles across 2 time steps ($T_0$ pre-event baseline and $T_1$ post-event pass) as a single $5\text{D}$ tensor: $(B, T=2, H=256, W=256, C=3)$.
STEP 2: SPATIAL ENCODER
2D Convolutions
Shared $32 \to 64 \to 128$ convolutional filters extract canopy texture, river valleys, terrain ridges, and unpaved logging roads independently for each image.
STEP 3: TEMPORAL MEMORY
ConvLSTM2D Delta Gating
A recurrent convolutional memory cell compares $T_0$ and $T_1$. It ignores harmless seasonal tree leaf changes and isolates true charcoal burns and clear-cut logging.
STEP 4: DAMAGE MAP OUTPUT
U-Net Transposed Decoder
Upsamples features with skip connections to output a precise $256 \times 256$ probability mask, which is vectorised into glowing GeoJSON damage polygons on the map.
